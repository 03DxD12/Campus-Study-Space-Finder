01

Project Overview & UX Project Brief

The project challenge, target audience, and research objective behind the Campus Study Space Finder.

Project Challenge

Why This Project Matters

Students at DLSP / PLSP struggle to locate available, comfortable study spaces during class gaps. The lack of a centralized information system forces trial-and-error roaming, wasting valuable study time and increasing frustration during peak hours (10 AM to 2 PM).

Target Users

Who We Studied

  • 1College students with irregular class schedules and frequent free periods between classes
  • 2Students enrolled at DLSP / PLSP, San Pablo City campus
  • 3Learners who need outlets, quiet zones, and reliable Wi-Fi for studying
Research Objective

What We Set Out to Learn

To identify the behavioral patterns, environmental pain points, and unmet needs of college students when searching for study spaces on campus, in order to inform the design of a real-time campus study space locator application.

Concept Preview

Study Space Locator App

A mobile-first app concept designed to remove every friction point students face when searching for a comfortable place to study.

Live Occupancy Status

Color-coded availability indicators (green / amber / red) show real-time crowd levels for every registered space on campus.

Quiet-Zone & Comfort Filters

Filter spaces by noise level, air conditioning, ventilation rating, and temperature. Find the right environment before you walk there.

Outlet & Amenity Indicators

Each space listing shows available outlet count, table surfaces, Wi-Fi quality, and whether the space allows food and drinks.

Peak-Hour Alerts

Proactive notifications warn students before peak hours begin (10 AM) suggesting the best available spaces to head to early.

Critical Issues Found

1

Filter icon not discoverable

Participants initially overlooked the filter icon in the top-right corner and expected a prominent "Filter" control below the search bar.

Critical
2

"Directions" CTA buried in detail page

Participants scrolled past the Directions control during testing. The button lacked sufficient contrast and visual hierarchy.

Critical

Positive Observations

✓

Color-coded status instantly understood

All participants understood the green/amber/red occupancy indicators without instruction: "Parang traffic light lang" (like a traffic light).

✓

Space cards praised for information density

Participants appreciated seeing seats, temperature, and outlet count at a glance. "Gusto ko 'to — lahat makikita agad!" (I like this — you can see everything right away!)

✓

App concept rated highly needed

All 3 participants described the concept as relevant to a recurring campus problem and indicated that they would use the app.

10

Design Iteration, Before & After

Changes made to the prototype based on usability testing findings, with rationale for each decision.

01

Filter Button: Icon → Labeled Pill Row

Usability finding: participants overlooked the icon-only filter. Severity: Critical.

❌ Before (v1)

⊞

Filter was a small icon in the top-right corner — easily overlooked, no label

✅ After (v2)

All Quiet Outlets A/C

Replaced with a visible horizontal chip row — immediately tappable, labeled, and scannable

Design Rationale: Labeled filter chips make available options visible without requiring users to interpret an unfamiliar icon. Moving filters inline also reduces the interaction from two taps to one.

02

Directions CTA: Small Link → Sticky Full-Width Button

Usability finding: participants missed the directions link. Severity: Critical.

❌ Before (v1)

Get directions →

Small inline text link, easily scrolled past

✅ After (v2)

📍 Get Directions

Sticky full-width button at the bottom of the detail card — impossible to miss

Design Rationale: Primary CTAs should be visually dominant and thumb-reachable. Changing from text link to a full-width sticky button follows iOS and Android design guidelines for primary actions on detail screens.

11

Final Solution, Reflection & Future Improvements

The resulting concept, the boundaries of the current evidence, and the next steps required to validate and extend the solution.

Final Solution

The final prototype is designed to reduce trial-and-error searching by centralizing occupancy, environmental conditions, amenities, and directions before students begin walking to a location.

Limitations

  • Limited sample size: the survey included N=27 respondents, while usability testing involved N=3 participants; findings should be validated with a larger population
  • Prototype tested on Figma, not a live system — occupancy data is simulated
  • Campus infrastructure (sensors/IoT) needed for real-time data collection

Future Improvements

  • Integrate with school ID card scanners for crowd-sourced occupancy tracking
  • Add seat reservation and "I'm leaving soon" notification features
  • Expand to other DLSP / PLSP buildings and multi-campus support
  • Accessibility audit (screen reader, contrast, large-text mode)
