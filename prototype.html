Thumb-Zone Ergonomics Analysis

Designed for Real-Life One-Handed Walking

College students walking between classes often hold a smartphone in one hand while carrying heavy backpacks or books. Our UI divides the screen into 3 intentional thumb-reach zones:

1
Header Zone (Easy Reach)

Sticky global search bar and horizontal category chip row positioned within natural upper thumb arc for fast filtering while moving.

2
Body Zone (Comfortable Scanning)

Vertical feed prioritized by real-time distance and seat availability. Key metrics (occupancy ratios, noise, temperature) are exposed on thumbnail cards to eliminate unnecessary taps.

3
Bottom Zone (Natural Thumb Zone)

Bottom Action Sheet drawer (Directions, Save, See Details, Share) and persistent 4-tab bar anchored at the lower viewport edge for zero-stretch one-handed reach.

Stages 9 & 10 Usability Validation

Moderated Testing & Design Iterations

Tested with N=3 DLSP / PLSP college students. System Usability Scale (SUS) Score: 87.5 / 100 (Grade A).

Interaction Component Before (V1 Initial Wireframe) After (V2 Final Implementation) Human-Centered Rationale
Filter Discovery Hidden inside sub-menu behind icon Visible horizontal labeled category chips Eliminates menu navigation while walking. Reduced tap depth to 1 tap.
Directions Navigation Small inline text link in drawer Full-width sticky Forest Green primary CTA button Core action becomes visually dominant and easily reachable by thumb.
Environmental Data Enclosed inside secondary detail drawer Micro-badges (24°C, 4 Outlets) visible on thumbnails Allows immediate visual scanning across campus spots without opening drawers.

Interactive Prototype & Cross-Platform Design

The linked interaction model and responsive considerations used to carry the core experience across mobile, tablet, and desktop contexts.

Mobile First

Primary actions remain thumb-reachable, controls use touch-friendly targets, and study-space information is prioritized for quick scanning.

Tablet Adaptation

Additional width supports a split view with search results beside space details while preserving the same task sequence.

Desktop Web

Map and list content can appear together, with filters retained in view to support faster comparison across campus locations.
